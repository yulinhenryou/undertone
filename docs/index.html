Undertone

Join the chorus, a little at a time.

At karaoke, joining a song usually means taking the mic. Undertone lets you bring your voice in one turn of the watch’s side wheel at a time, from listening to singing beside the lead, and step back out whenever you like.

01The moment

Karaoke has two volumes: silent and spotlight.

Someone passes you the song. Take the mic and you’re the main act. Stay quiet and you’re out of it. Singing along without a mic is a guess: you can’t tell how much you stand out, or whether you’re drowning out the singer.

Take the micEveryone is listening to you.
Sing without itYou have no idea how loud you are.
UndertoneThe volumes in between.

02The idea

Four positions. One wheel.

Each turn of the side wheel moves your voice one step further into the song, and each step says what the room now hears. Turn it back down to step out. The screen warms with you.

03The one rule

Only you can
turn yourself up.

The room may turn you down, or switch you off. Never up.

Room gets loud

Held at Undertone

Harmony and Duet drop to Undertone while it lasts. When it clears, you stay put, and may turn up.

Song is solo

Joining closes

The lead sets it before she sings, and the watch says why. Nobody is turned away in public.

Connection drops

Off, and stays off

Reconnecting never reopens your mic. You decide when to join again.

Wrist down

Song ends

Back to listening

09Sketching

Five decisions, sketched before any screen.

Kept
Sheet 1 · Input

Five ways to control joining, drawn by hand. Stepped turns of the side wheel won: turning maps to “more or less of me”, the steps can be counted by feel, and nobody sees you do it.

Input Kept: wheel steps

A slider needs a look; a join toggle is all or nothing.

2

Layout Kept: one word

A ring read as a volume meter, a list as a menu.

3

Visibility Kept: private

Who is singing is never shown or counted, on any screen.

4

Naming Cut

Naming the person who joins added risk and no benefit.

5

Leaving Changed

The palm cover I ticked can’t be promised on watchOS. Turning down became the exit, plus a button.

After testing · not yet tested

The scale now ends beside the lead.

In testing, one participant reached Harmony and tried one more turn. The level bar already had a third segment that only the cut duet ever used. So the duet came back as the top step, without a name, reached one turn at a time like every other level, and the screen now warms as you rise.

The wheel steps stayed as they were: one mouse scroll skipped a level, but a mouse says little about a real watch wheel.

13Outro

What I’d keep, and what’s still open.

Why a watch

Already on your wrist

A phone has to be unlocked and held up; a shared tablet makes joining public. The cost is a tiny screen with no room to explain, which is why every word mattered.

The decision that mattered

Control moved to the joiner

Moving it from the singer’s tablet to the joiner’s wrist changed who the design serves. One rule then shaped every other screen.

What critique changed

I stopped adding

Extra features competed with joining. Testing showed people understood the levels and stumbled on words, so I fixed the words.

A trade-off I kept

The native look

I expect an Apple-like screen to feel trustworthy on a watch, though that’s untested. The touch version stays separate so the default screen stays quiet.

Still open

How loud each level sounds to othersReal wheel steps on a watchWhether friends recognise a voiceSeparating neighbouring voicesDuet and the stage light with usersWhether Duet should need the lead’s OKVoiceOver on a real watch
